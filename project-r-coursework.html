Regression and hypothesis testing in R

Coursework  |  Econometrics and statistics

The question

How does hotel occupancy respond to temperature in different cities, and what do earnings data show about gender, height, and age?

What I did

  1. Hotel occupancy regression (Econometrics): fit a pooled regression of hotel occupancy on temperature, tested differences between three cities with regression and ANOVA, then added city indicators and temperature-by-city interactions.
  2. Built the design matrix by hand with dummy variables and interaction terms to confirm it matched the model R estimates.
  3. Constructed confidence intervals for the temperature effect in each city from the regression coefficients and covariance matrix.
  4. Earnings and height statistics project: calculated summary statistics, ran t-tests on the mean age and on the earnings gap between men and women, tested the correlation between height and earnings, and tested hypotheses about variances.

Results

Hotel occupancy and temperature

  • The pooled regression found almost no relationship between temperature and occupancy (a slope of about 0.08 points per degree, with an R² near zero).
  • Splitting by city showed why: in Davos, occupancy fell by about 2.7 points for each extra degree, in Polenca it rose by about 4.0 points, and in Basel the effect was small (about 1.3).
  • The model with city interactions explained about 54% of the variation in occupancy. The takeaway is that pooling cities with opposite seasonal patterns hides real effects.

Earnings and height

  • About 55.8% of people in the dataset were female, and 534 people were mechanics.
  • I rejected a mean age of 40 at the 5% level; the 95% confidence interval for age was [40.77, 41.07].
  • Mean earnings were $48,459.75 for men and $45,621 for women, a difference that was statistically significant.
  • Height and earnings had a weak but statistically significant positive correlation of about 0.10 (95% confidence interval [0.090, 0.119]).
  • I could not reject that the variance of earnings equals 727,000,000 (p = 0.78), or that men and women have equal earnings variances (p = 0.68).

Code preview

Pooled, per-city, and interaction models

# Pooled model: does temperature predict hotel occupancy?
a <- lm(hotel_Occupation ~ Temperature, data = hotel)

# Separate model for one city
Davos_m <- lm(hotel_Occupation ~ Temperature, data = filter(hotel, City == "Davos"))

# Allow the temperature effect to differ by city
g <- lm(hotel_Occupation ~ Temperature * City, data = hotel)
summary(g)
confint(g, level = 0.99)
