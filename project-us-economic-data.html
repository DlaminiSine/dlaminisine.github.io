US economic data analysis with the FRED API

Python notebook  |  Data from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED)

The question

How did unemployment and labor force participation compare across US states, and how did they move together around 2020 to 2021?

  1. FRED API
  2. →
  3. State unemployment series (loop)
  4. +
  5. State participation series
  6. →
  7. One combined dataframe
  8. →
  9. State comparisons and charts

What I did

  1. Connected to the FRED API with the fredapi library and searched its catalog for economic series.
  2. Pulled the S&P 500 as a first time series and plotted it.
  3. Collected unemployment rates for every state: searched for the monthly, seasonally adjusted state series, looped over the series IDs, and combined them into one dataframe with the state names as columns.
  4. Pulled labor force participation rates for the states the same way.
  5. Compared unemployment and participation with a grid of charts for every state for 2020 to 2021, and a closer look at California.

Results

  • August 2023 unemployment by state: Puerto Rico (about 6.2%), Nevada (about 5.4%), and the District of Columbia (about 5.0%) had the highest rates. Maryland, Vermont, and New Hampshire had the lowest, under 2%.
  • California, 2020 to 2021: unemployment rose sharply in spring 2020 and then fell steadily, while labor force participation dipped and then recovered.

Code preview

Pulling every state's unemployment series from FRED

unemp_df = fred.search('unemployment rate state', filter=('frequency', 'Monthly'))
unemp_df = unemp_df.query('seasonal_adjustment == "Seasonally Adjusted" and units == "Percent"')

all_results = []
for myid in unemp_df.index:
    results = fred.get_series(myid)
    results = results.to_frame(name=myid)
    all_results.append(results)
    time.sleep(0.1)

unemp_results = pd.concat(all_results, axis=1)
